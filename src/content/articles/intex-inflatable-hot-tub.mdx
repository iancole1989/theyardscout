## What you're actually buying — the Intex PureSpa platform

Intex is one of the two giants of the inflatable spa world (Bestway being the other). The PureSpa range is their entire hot tub line, and it's built around a few consistent design choices:

- **Fiber-Tech construction** — the sidewalls use a 3-ply laminated material with thousands of high-strength polyester fibers laminated between PVC layers. In plain terms: the walls are firmer and more rigid than a basic blow-up ring. You can perch on the edge without it folding under you, which is a real, noticeable difference from cheaper inflatables.
- **Built-in hard-water treatment** — a calcium-reducing system in the pump that softens incoming water and reduces scale buildup on the heater element and liner. This is genuinely Intex's best-kept advantage and we'll come back to it.
- **All-in-one pump unit** — heater, filtration, and bubble/jet generation in one external pad-mounted unit, the same architecture as SaluSpa.
- **104°F maximum temperature** — the standard regulatory cap. Every consumer inflatable spa tops out here; nobody runs hotter legally.

The PureSpa platform is sold under model names that rotate year to year, but they all sit on this same chassis. The practical buying decision is mostly about which *features* you want bolted onto it.

## The Intex PureSpa lineup — how the models break down

The range divides cleanly into three buckets. Names and exact SKUs shift annually, so shop by feature set, not by the marketing name on the box.

### Bubble-only tier — PureSpa / PureSpa Plus

- **140 high-powered bubble jets** around the base, the standard air-massage setup
- **4-person** (~57in inner / 77in outer diameter, ~210 gallon capacity) and **6-person** (~65in inner / 85in outer) configurations
- Energy-efficient insulated cover included
- Roughly the budget-to-mid price band — typically the cheapest way into the Intex line. **Check current price**, as it swings a lot by retailer and season.

The **PureSpa Plus** is the step up most buyers land on — same 140-bubble system but with upgraded touches like better insulation, a removable/wireless control panel on some model years, and bundled headrests. It's the volume seller in the range.

### Bubble + LED tier — Greywood Deluxe

- Same 140-bubble air system, dressed up with **multi-color LED lighting**, foam headrests, and a grey exterior finish
- Some model years add the **INTEX Link app** for phone control of temperature and bubbles, plus a removable wireless-charging control panel
- 4-person and 6-person versions
- Mid price band — you're paying for the LED ambiance and app, not for a fundamentally different spa

### Jet + bubble tier — PureSpa Plus Jet & Bubble Deluxe

- This is the one that adds **actual water jets** on top of the bubble system, for a deeper, more directed massage than air bubbles alone
- Larger footprint, digital control panel managing temperature, bubbles, and jets independently
- 6-person square and round versions exist depending on year
- Top of the range — **check current price**, this tier carries the biggest premium

If you specifically want water-jet hydromassage rather than just air bubbles, the Jet & Bubble Deluxe is the only Intex model that delivers it. Everything below it is bubbles only — which most owners are perfectly happy with, but it's a real distinction worth understanding before you buy.

## Are Intex hot tubs good? The honest answer

Yes — with one asterisk. As an inflatable spa, the Intex PureSpa is a well-made product that does what it claims. The Fiber-Tech walls are firmer than most rivals, the hard-water treatment is a real feature SaluSpa doesn't match cleanly, setup genuinely takes about 20 minutes, and the heat retention on the Plus models is competitive.

The asterisk is the pump. And it's a meaningful one.

### The reliability reality you need to plan around

Intex PureSpa has a documented pattern of **premature pump and heater failures**. As of 2026 there is an active legal investigation by a US consumer-rights firm into reports of PureSpa pumps and heaters failing early, plus water and air leaks. That doesn't mean every unit fails — plenty run for years — but it means you should price the spa as if the pump is a **2-to-3-year wear part**, not a lifetime component. That's actually true of every inflatable spa, Intex and SaluSpa alike, but the Intex failure reports are loud enough that we won't paper over them.

The other recurring headache is the **E81 error code**. This is a wireless-connectivity fault between the removable control panel and the pump base — it can be triggered by a low panel battery, the panel sitting too far from the base, a tripped RCD on the plug, or a genuine motor fault. It's the single most-Googled Intex PureSpa problem. Most cases clear with a re-pair or RCD reset, but a stubborn minority of owners report it returning, which usually points at a failing pump.

## Intex PureSpa vs Bestway SaluSpa — the head-to-head

This is the comparison that matters, because these two own the category. They're closer than most buyers assume.

| Factor | Intex PureSpa | Bestway SaluSpa |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sidewall build** | Fiber-Tech 3-ply, firmer walls — slight edge | Tritech 3-ply, solid but slightly softer feel |
| **Hard-water handling** | Built-in hard-water treatment system — real advantage | No equivalent built-in system on most models |
| **Model range** | Narrower — bubble, LED, and one jet model | Broader — more sizes, more model variants |
| **Pump reliability** | Documented failure reports + active investigation | Pump fails too, but a deeper aftermarket parts ecosystem |
| **Parts availability** | Good, but fewer aftermarket third-party options | Best-in-class — biggest aftermarket support |
| **Price** | Often slightly cheaper at the same tier | Competitive, varies by retailer |
| **Water jets** | Only on Jet & Bubble Deluxe | Only on HydroJet variants |

**Where Intex wins:** the Fiber-Tech walls feel a touch more rigid, and the hard-water treatment is a legitimate advantage if you're filling from a hard-water supply — it slows the calcium scale that kills heater elements. If your tap water leaves white crust on your kettle, Intex's built-in softening is a real reason to lean Intex.

**Where SaluSpa wins:** the broader model range and the deeper aftermarket parts ecosystem. When your SaluSpa pump dies, there are more third-party replacement and repair options than for Intex. For a full breakdown of the SaluSpa side, see our [Coleman SaluSpa guide](/coleman-saluspa/) — note that Coleman SaluSpa is just Bestway under a licensing badge, so it's the same platform.

The honest verdict: **buy whichever is cheaper at the tier and size you want**, unless you have hard water (lean Intex) or you want the widest model selection and best repair support (lean SaluSpa). The quality gap is small in both directions.

## Hard water — Intex's quiet strength

Worth its own section because it's underrated and it's the clearest reason to pick Intex over SaluSpa.

In a hard-water area, dissolved calcium and magnesium precipitate out as scale when water is heated. In an inflatable spa, that scale coats the heater element (making it work harder and fail sooner) and leaves chalky residue on the liner. It's one of the quietest killers of inflatable spa pumps.

Intex's **built-in hard-water treatment system** sits in the pump and reduces the dissolved hardness before it hits the heater. It's not a full water softener and you'll still want to manage your water chemistry, but it measurably slows scale buildup — which directly extends heater life and keeps the water gentler on skin. If you already know your area runs hard water, this single feature is a stronger argument for Intex than anything in the marketing copy.

## Capacity — read the person count with skepticism

Like every inflatable spa, Intex's stated capacity is optimistic by about one person:

- **4-person PureSpa** (~210 gallons) — comfortable for **2-3 adults**. Four adults is a squeeze with knees touching.
- **6-person PureSpa** — comfortable for **4-5 adults**. Six is technically possible, realistically cramped.

The constraint isn't water volume, it's seating geometry — inflatable spas have no molded seats, so everyone sits on the floor against the wall, and adult shoulders take up more arc than the spec sheet assumes. Buy one size up from your honest regular headcount. For help sizing across the whole category, our [best inflatable hot tub guide](/best-inflatable-hot-tub/) walks through capacity by use case.

## Operating cost — comparable to the rest of the category

Intex runs on the same external-pump architecture as SaluSpa, so running costs land in the same band. The biggest swing factor is climate and how well your cover insulates.

| Climate | Monthly all-in (electricity + chemicals + filters) |
| --- | --- |
| Mild summer | $40-65 |
| Temperate | $55-90 |
| Cold winter | $90-150 |
| Severe cold | $130-200 |

The PureSpa Plus models' upgraded insulation and the included energy-efficient cover help at the margins, but physics is physics — an inflatable spa loses heat through thin walls faster than a hard-sided unit. Running year-round in a cold climate is where inflatables get expensive. See the [inflatable hot tub guide](/inflatable-hot-tub/) for the full operating-cost breakdown and cover-upgrade economics.

## Lifespan and parts

Plan for the same lifecycle as the broader inflatable category, with Intex's reliability reports nudging the pump estimate to the cautious end:

- **Pump unit**: 2-3 years typical, sometimes less given the failure reports. The pump is the component that ends most inflatable spas.
- **Liner / sidewalls**: 3-5 years with care; Fiber-Tech walls hold up well to normal use but are still puncturable.
- **Most owners replace the whole unit at year 3-4** rather than chasing repairs.

Filter cartridges are cheap and widely available (and cross-compatible across much of the PureSpa range). Whole-pump replacements are available but the third-party aftermarket is thinner than SaluSpa's — one of the few places SaluSpa's scale advantage shows up in daily ownership.

## Bottom line

The Intex PureSpa is a legitimately good inflatable hot tub. The Fiber-Tech walls are firmer than most rivals, the built-in hard-water treatment is a real and underrated advantage, and the bubble massage does the job for the price. Against Bestway SaluSpa it's a near tie — Intex edges ahead on walls and hard water, SaluSpa edges ahead on model range and parts support.

The one thing not to ignore is pump reliability. Buy it knowing the pump is a 2-3 year wear part, keep your warranty paperwork, and you'll get good value. Want a 10-year appliance? No inflatable is your answer — that's a hard-sided spa conversation.
