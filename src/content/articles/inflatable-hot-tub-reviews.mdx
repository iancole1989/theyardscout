## How we rate inflatable hot tubs

Before the capsule reviews, here's the lens. We weight four things, in this order:

1. **Real comfortable capacity** — how many adults actually relax in it, not the box number.
2. **Water volume and running cost** — more water means more money to heat and maintain, every month.
3. **Reliability over time** — specifically how the tub holds up around the year-two mark when pumps tend to go.
4. **Value for the use case** — the right tub for a couple is the wrong tub for a family of five.

What we *don't* over-weight: jet counts (above a point they're marketing), color, and out-of-the-box wow factor. A tub that's delightful on day one and dead at month 20 isn't a good tub. For the full methodology and lifecycle data, see our [inflatable hot tub buying guide](/inflatable-hot-tub/).

## SaluSpa Coronado AirJet — best all-rounder

**The pick for most buyers.** The Coronado is the value sweet spot of the SaluSpa lineup: rated 4–6 person (realistically two adults comfortable, a tight three), around 140 AirJets, and roughly 297 gallons — enough water for proper shoulder-deep submersion, which the smaller tubs can't always manage. It's the model we point most general buyers toward because it gets the fundamentals right without overreaching on size or price.

The trade-offs are the category's, not the Coronado's: it's a bubble-jet tub (no targeted water jets), the pump carries the usual ~18–30 month risk, and at ~300 gallons it's not cheap to keep hot in cold weather. But for a couple or small household who wants one tub that does the job, it's the easy recommendation. ## SaluSpa Miami AirJet — best budget entry

**The pick for the lowest sane entry price.** The Miami is the smaller, cheaper SaluSpa — rated 2–4 person (honestly two adults), lower fill volume than the Coronado, and a correspondingly lower running cost. It's the tub to buy when you want the inflatable experience for as little as possible and you're a couple, not a crowd. The smaller volume is a feature here: it heats faster and costs less to maintain.

Where it falls short is room. Two adults fit comfortably; a third is a squeeze, and you don't get the deep-water feel of the Coronado. The pump and freeze limits are identical to the rest of the line. For couples on a budget it's a genuinely sensible buy — our full [Bestway SaluSpa Miami review](/soaking-up-the-good-times-a-review-of-the-bestway-saluspa-miami-inflatable-hot-tub/) covers it in depth.

## Coleman SaluSpa — same tub, different badge

**Not a separate product.** "Coleman" on an inflatable hot tub is a licensed brand applied to Bestway's SaluSpa hardware. A Coleman SaluSpa and the equivalent Bestway SaluSpa are the same pump, the same liner, the same lifecycle — the badge is the only difference. So our Coleman "review" is really a pricing note: buy whichever of the two is cheaper at the moment for the model you want, and don't pay a premium or take a discount based on the name. **The main alternative to the SaluSpa world.** Intex's PureSpa line is the chief competitor to Bestway, and the small 2-person PureSpa bubble model is our top pick for couples specifically — smallest footprint, lowest fill volume, cheapest to heat, and easy to fit on a tight patio or even indoors. The PureSpa line also scales up to larger models, but its real edge is at the small end, where it's the cleanest true-two-person option. ## What inflatable hot tub reviews consistently get wrong

If you read enough ratings, the same blind spots repeat. Avoid them:

- **They trust the "X-person" rating.** A "6-person" tub seats three adults comfortably. Reviews that recommend a tub for a family of six are setting buyers up for a cramped disappointment. Halve the number.
- **They ignore running cost.** A glowing review that never mentions the monthly electricity bill to keep ~300 gallons hot is missing the second-biggest factor in ownership satisfaction.
- **They grade out of the box.** Day-one impressions are easy to love. The review that matters is the one written at month 22 when the pump starts struggling — that's where these tubs are actually decided.
- **They pretend the models are radically different.** Most are the same Bestway platform. A review hyping a tub as "far better built" than its siblings is usually hyping a badge.
- **They skip the freeze problem.** Standard inflatables stop heating effectively below ~40°F ambient and aren't truly freeze-safe unheated. A four-season claim without that caveat is a red flag.

## The shared failure profile (read this before any single review)

Every model above shares the same three weaknesses. Internalize these and you'll never be surprised:

- **Pump failure around 18–30 months.** This is the defining flaw of the category and the most common owner complaint, full stop. It's not a Coronado problem or an Intex problem — it's an inflatable hot tub problem. Budget for a replacement pump or a warranty claim near year two, and favor retailers with forgiving returns (Costco, when stocked).
- **Overstated capacity.** Roughly half the rated number is the real comfortable count, across every brand.
- **No true freeze protection.** Below ~40°F ambient, standard inflatables can't keep up, and left unheated in a freeze they can be damaged. For serious winter use, insulated EnergySense models plus accessories are the only honest path — see our [best inflatable hot tub for winter](/best-inflatable-hot-tub-for-winter/) guide.
